# Guild Member

## Requirements
You must physically visit one of the Guild's facilities and formally register to be part of the Guild. Different Guilds may have different requirements for entry:
- Some may cater to specific professions, requiring you to have Received an appropriate Skill or be Wearing an appropriate Hat.
- Some may require an entry fee, typically in the form of sacrificing 1 to 2 dots of Money or similar.
- Some may require you to participate in some kind of initiation, orientation, or challenge in order to qualify for guild membership.

When you start Wearing this Hat, write down what kind of guild you signed up for. In some cases, Guilds may expect you to only be a member of one Guild at a time.

## Powers

### Guild Resources
Guilds provide certain convenient services and facilities to their members. These vary from Guild to Guild, but all are represented by dots of Guild Resources.

When you start Wearing this Hat, gain 1 dot of the Source 'Guild Resources' for free; this Source represents your standing within the Guild and your access to its services. You can buy Guild Resources like any other Source. Guild Resources can be part of a roll when you are making use of the facilities of your Guild, or calling in favors from Guild members.

### Guild Allies
You may purchase dots of the source 'Guild Allies' while you are a member of a Guild. This Source represents social connections within the Guild on a more personal level--people you've worked directly with, people who owe you favors, people who've taken a liking to you or respect your accomplishments. The GM is encouraged to make these NPCs a recurring part of your story.

### Licenses
Some Guilds are regulatory bodies with the power to grant licenses for performing certain kinds of work. In this case, treat a Guild License as a Unique Item, writing down what sorts of things the license permits. For instance, an Adventurer's Guild may regulate who gets what quests by issuing Licenses that describe the level of skill an Adventurer holds, whereas a Thieve's Guild might issue licenses for specialized tools and a Mage's Guild may regulate access to forbidden magic through licenses.

## Progression

Guilds do not have any special progression mechanics, aside from acquiring Licenses through the usual Unique Item rules.

## Risks

### Membership Dues
Guilds typically require that their members pay a fee to fund Guild services. Once per month, make a Simple Item roll (see core rulebook, "Items and Item Rolls") using Money. Failing this roll means you start falling behind on your membership dues--gain a dot of the Negative Source 'Guild Debt'. Guild Debt applies as a dice penalty on the roll to pay your Membership Dues, so it adds up fast!

If you accrue enough Guild Debt, the Guild may start making additional demands, may restrict your access to their facilities, or may even outright ban you from the Guild.

### Guild Quests
Guilds may offer (or demand) special tasks for their members, called Guild Quests. These tasks typically carry some degree of risk for the members of the Guild who take them on, but are also associated with rewards--either in the form of further access to Guild Resources, or in straight-up Money or Complex/Unique items.

### Guild Politics
Not everything runs smoothly in the day-to-day operation of a Guild. Some characters may consider your presence within a Guild to be a challenge to their authority. Some may simply feel you rub them the wrong way, and take that out on you. Whatever the case, it is seemingly inevitable that *someone* will start maneuvering in the shadows...

Your foes within Guild Politics typically don't want you *dead*, and often consider outright physical confrontation to be beneath them, but that doesn't mean they won't  ruin your day. They may attempt to steal dots of Guild Resources or Guild Allies from you, or even try to get you banned from the Guild outright! This takes time and effort, though, especially if you've made a name for yourself as a reliable practicioner of your craft.
