# Hacker

You are an expert in information warfare and systems analysis. If someone needs an electronic lock opened, a server shut down, or a secret revealed, they call you.

## Requirements

- Received one dot in Hacker

## Powers

### Daemons
Daemons are pre-made effects that you build ahead of time. You roll the dice when you create them, and then note down the successes you got and what they were for; later, you can trigger the Daemon, consuming one of its uses and getting its effects when you need them. Daemons are the main thing that gives Hackers an advantage, since you can have any number of them ready to go for any given occasion.

Daemons have limited uses to represent exploits being discovered and patched, having to stop before a security program discovers you, raw dumb luck getting in the way, and all sorts of other reasons a program might fail in the wild. Use whatever justification makes sense to you.

To make a Daemon, you need at least a day of free time.

First, declare what you are trying to accomplish with this Daemon, and write it down. Maybe this Daemon is meant to augment your homemade heads-up display to give you better targeting during a fight, or maybe it's designed for breaking a keypad lock's code, or maybe it's meant to shut down all the biometric-locked guns in a building. The GM may give you bonuses for being specific or for easy tasks, or penalties for harder tasks, and they reserve the right to say that what you're doing is impossible ("The guns aren't biometric-locked").

Then, roll Hacker with whatever makes sense. Mainframe is a good one, if you have it (see Mainframe below). You can roll once per day you spend on the task (with one exception, see below). Keep track of how many successes you got. If you don't have any successes when you're done rolling, you've been stumped--but if you can spend more time on the project, you can keep rolling.

Finally, decide how many uses you'll get out of this daemon. You always get at least one use, but you can spend successes from your rolls to add additional uses, at a cost of 2 successes to 1 extra use. Write down the number of successes you have after all this; this is your Daemon's 'rating'. Also write down how many uses this Daemon has. Each time you trigger the Daemon, you remove one use and trigger its effects, as if you had just made a roll and gotten the Daemon's rating in successes.

#### All-Nighters
Hackers are somewhat infamous for ignoring their physical health to finish this one last feature... You can choose to pull All-Nighters when working on a Daemon. Each day that you do this lets you roll twice that day. However, when you pull an All-Nighter, any dice that *didn't* come up as successes instead give you dots of a Temporary Negative Source called Exhaustion, which are a penalty on any roll for mental tasks and can be removed one-at-a-time by getting a good night's sleep. Yes, programming *is* a mental task, so a really bad all-nighter can leave you unable to do meaningful work on a Daemon until you sleep it off...

#### Augmenting a Daemon
You can choose to work on a Daemon you've already created. Roll as normal for making a Daemon, and add the results to the existing Daemon's rating and/or uses.

### Mainframes
Computers are crazy powerful these days, but Mainframes are more powerful still. A Mainframe is an essential tool for pushing the limits of computing, and for the sort of Hacker who does under-the-table work, securing a Mainframe is the first step to the big leagues.

As a Hacker, any dots of Mainframe (Source) you have can be used whenever you have an internet connection--to run calculations, brute-force a password, search corners of the internet no search engine will touch, and so on. Mainframes are also a convenient place to store your Daemons, for the same reason.

Mainframes you control are more difficult to break into--the reason it takes a thief to catch a thief is that thieves know which windows to put bars over. You'll get a notification if your Mainframe is under attack via the internet, and you can take a Reaction (and/or trigger a Daemon) in response to this. You'll also get a notification if someone physically breaks into the building your Mainframe is in...but there are limits to what you can do if they're physically present and you *aren't*.

### Combat Hacking

Hacking in the middle of combat is a stressful process, and takes up your turn the same way any attack action would. Furthermore, you can only hack things that are computerized and attached to the network in some way--in other words, you can only hack the bad guy's gun if the bad guy's gun has wifi. This doesn't mean it's *never* an option, just that you need to ask the GM what things you can manipulate from here. (The GM is encouraged to give you at least *something* to work with.)

In some settings, cybernetic augmentations exist, and these tend to be networked, as a way of being able to run diagnostics and do basic software tasks like maintenance and updates without having to cut you open again. This means that augmentations are naturally vulnerable to hacking, though you may need to get a bit closer than you'd like.

You can *also* use your software skills to optimize equipment you and your allies have...but this requires network access, too, so being able to do this opens you up to hostile hacker activity. Think of it like close air support...he who controls the network, can ruin the other poor bastard's day.

## Progression

Hackers are defined by what they do--what they've accomplished. If you acheive a Goal that has something to do with hacking, in addition to the effects of achieving a Goal, you also gain a dot of Hacker Rep (Source). Hacker Rep is a representation of your renown within the cybersecurity community, whether you're on the right side of the law or not. In addition to the usual uses for Sources that represent social clout, you can add your Hacker Rep as a bonus to any Hacker roll. However, **as long as you wear this Hat, you cannot gain Hacker Rep in any way but by achieving Goals**.

## Risks

### Government Agents
If you spend a lot of time committing cybercrime, eventually some of it is gonna get traced back to you. Government agents may attempt to physically raid your home, workplace, or whatever building your Mainframes are in; the consequences of this depend on which place they hit, whether you're there when they do, and what happens while they're doing the raid. Obviously, they'll try to arrest you, and if they get physical access to a Mainframe they'll take it offline--or worse, sneak some spyware onto it and track *you* down...

Being on the right side of the law doesn't make you much safer. Government agents will *also* try to recruit hackers who seem interested in staying on the up-and-up, especially if said hackers are making waves with massive security discoveries--and there may come a time when these agents make you an 'offer' you can't refuse. Choosing to work with the government is an option, but a lot of hackers have beef with the government for various reasons, and it'll color your interactions with other hackers for the rest of your career.

### Corporate Agents
Some corporations don't wanna wait for silly things like 'due process' or 'rules of engagement'. Corporate agents, if they take an interest in you, are similar to Government agents--but they're usually more aggressive, less interested in operating legally, and somehow have even *better* funding than the cops.

### Other Hackers
The hacking community is welcoming, and usually doesn't do a lot of infighting...but that doesn't mean you'll never be facing off against another Hacker. The reason could be anything--maybe they were hired by a corporation you attacked a while ago, maybe you're the one hired by the corp and they're the one trying to break in. Maybe they're some upstart who wants to get a quick rep boost by taking down a legend. Hell, maybe they have no idea that someone already took over this Mainframe.

### Physical Access Is Root Access
Finally, there's one simple ironclad rule that always holds true: No matter how expensive the security on your Mainframe is, a five dollar wrench will beat the password out of you. If someone's really desperate to stop you, they *will* take it to meatspace, and hacking skills can't stop a chunk of dumb steel.
