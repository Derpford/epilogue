# Broker
You know who to call, what to offer, and what you can get away with. If someone needs something, you can facilitate that deal.

## Requirements

- Received one dot of Broker, Dealer, or another Skill that involves moving money around
OR
- Have five combined dots of Money and Connections

## Powers

### Power Brokering
You can set up deals between entities. Whether those entities are corporations, mobsters, governments, government contractors, or your fellow partners in crime--that's up to you. You can even be part of these deals yourself, though you might wanna be careful about that...

To broker power, start by declaring what you want in terms of an Attribute, and what you're offering (also in the form of an Attribute). Describe how you want to achieve this. For example, you could broker for dots of Beefy for an ally, in the form of experimental (and likely stolen from a lab) augmentations. You might instead be looking for Money, and be roping someone into a less-than-legitimate business deal, job 'offer', or--let's face it--maybe you're straight up gonna sell parts of their body. It's an option on the table!

If the GM rules that your plan is possible, roll with one of Broker, Connections, or Money. (You can choose the other part of the roll.) If you got at least one success, you sealed the deal, and can trade one dot of the payment for one dot of the product. If you offered Money for Beefy, you lose a dot of Money and gain a dot of Beefy.

Each additional success lets you trade an additional dot. You *cannot* gain multiple dots per dot spent, because you're not a miracle worker. The extra successes represent finding a buyer with deeper pockets or a seller with more (or better) product.

#### Cheating
You can also choose not to hold up your end of the deal.

This will have consequences. The GM will handle this part.

### Hirelings
You can also just straight-up hire specialists to do your dirty work. This is treated like a Unique Item roll, except that you can pay part of the cost upfront and part of it after the work's done, whether that cost is dots of Money or doing a quest as a favor to the guy you hired.

This works the same as creating a Unique Item, except that the 'item' is a person with thoughts, feelings, and autonomy. The sort of villainous acts which work around these restrictions is out of scope of this Hat. The upside of this is that Hirelings can act independently of you, which means you don't have to micromanage them--as long as you trust them, of course.

## Progression
Brokers live and die by their contacts and resources. They do not have any additional progression mechanics beyond the Depth that caps their Sources.

## Risks

### Deals Gone Bad
When you fail a Power Brokering roll, decide not to pay for your end of the deal, or fail to pay off a Hireling, you may piss someone off. This is bad. The consequences depend on who you pissed off and how, but they range from losing access to the kind of deal you were trying to make (your bad reputation precedes you), to having armed thugs show up to try and *take* what you owe.

### Offers You Can't Refuse
If you do a lot of deals with criminal organizations, you may find that they want...shall we say, *exclusivity* agreements. Likewise, you'll probably face pressure from local governments, whether that's pressure to *stop* or pressure to *work with them.* Naturally, the mob and the government are both willing to use force to get their way.

### Getting Cheated
You're not the only one who can cheat. The GM may inform you, after you've rolled and paid the cost, that whoever you were dealing with stood you up! You can still get your goods, but you'll have to break some kneecaps. The GM will tell you who screwed you. It's up to you to decide how to deal with them.
