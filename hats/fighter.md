# Fighter Hat

## Requirements
- Received 1 dot in Brawler, Duelist, or any other Skill that involves melee combat

## Powers

### Maneuvers
Maneuvers are a special kind of effect that can be triggered *after rolling, but before damage is dealt*. Maneuvers do not require additional rolls--instead, you spend successes from your combat rolls to trigger them, letting you divide your efforts between utility and damage. It's more consistent than Combo Actions, though you will find yourself agonizing over whether to commit to damage or to your other options…

You can only use a Maneuver once per attack, but many Maneuvers let you spend additional successes for additional effect.

### List of Maneuvers

#### Parry
You can use the best defense: a good offense. Spend any number of successes from your attack roll to give yourself that many dots of a Temporary Source called "Parry Stance", by striking to intercept their weapon or fists. Parry Stance applies as a bonus to Block and Redirect rolls, and can be used or burned as part of rolls with melee weapons.

#### Sweep
Spend 2 successes to try and knock your target down, adding 3 dots of a Temporary Negative Source called "Off-Balance"; it applies as a penalty to attack and reaction rolls.  If they already had Off-Balance, remove all their dots of Off-Balance; they are stunned for a turn from being knocked over.

#### Feint
Spend any number of successes to trick your opponent into overcommitting to defense. If they take a Defensive Action this round, reduce its effect by 1 per success you spent. If they don't take a Defensive Action this round, you instead gain 1 die on your next attack per success you spent.

## Progression
You may spend 3 XP to gain a new Maneuver from the List of Maneuvers.

## Risks

### Tournaments
Fighters of great renown may be invited to Tournaments. These can be above-board or underground, strictly regimented or no-holds-barred, and always feature a collection of similarly-skilled opponents for the Fighter to compete against; Tournaments may *also* include a powerful rival of some kind, an experienced foe who may become a recurring character in the fighter's journey.

While there's no real downside to just refusing every invitation, a top-tier Fighter may become embroiled in arena politics, as those who coordinate and bet on these Tournaments are always looking for ways to put their name behind a strong warrior, and those who *do* participate are often thirsty for a challenge (and may not have any qualms about coercing a challenger).

If you're especially well-renowned (or infamous), other warriors from across the world may actively seek you out for duels. You may wish to invest in convenient means of nonlethally dispatching surprise attackers, if you don't like the idea of constantly having to kill promising young students of the art of combat simply because they thought you'd make a good goal.
